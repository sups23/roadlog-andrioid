# Road Traffic Injuries in Nepal — Public Health Literature

## Citation
Various authors. Search in journals such as *WHO South-East Asia Journal of Public Health*, *International Journal of Injury Control and Safety Promotion*, *Journal of Nepal Health Research Council*, and *BMC Public Health*.

## Core purpose
These studies document the burden of road traffic injuries in Nepal, including risk factors, vulnerable groups, and healthcare impacts.

## What is useful for intersection observation research
- **Health framing:** Connects transport behaviour to injury and death.
- **Vulnerable groups:** Pedestrians, motorcyclists, and elderly are often highlighted.
- **Risk factors:** Speed, lack of crossings, poor lighting, and mixed traffic.
- **Intervention evidence:** What measures have reduced injuries elsewhere.

## Key takeaways for a Kathmandu project
- Strengthens the motivation for your observational study.
- Helps you identify which behaviours are most harmful.
- Provides a public-health justification beyond engineering.

## Limitations
- Often based on hospital or police records with limited detail.
- May not focus on intersection behaviour specifically.

## Suggested use
Use 1–2 public-health papers in your introduction to show the human cost of unsafe intersection behaviour.
