# Gehl — Cities for People

## Citation
Gehl, J. (2011). *Cities for People*. Washington, DC: Island Press.

## Core purpose
A practical guide to designing cities around human scale and pedestrian needs, based on decades of observation in Denmark and worldwide.

## What is useful for intersection observation research
- **Public life studies:** Methods for counting and observing people in public spaces.
- **Walking quality:** What makes walking comfortable, safe, and attractive.
- **Stationary activities:** Where people stop, stand, and wait.
- **Scale and speed:** How vehicle speed and road width affect pedestrian comfort.

## Key takeaways for a Kathmandu project
- Gehl’s observation methods can be adapted for bus stops and intersections.
- Helps you think about waiting, standing, and crossing as part of public life.
- Supports a human-centred critique of current intersection design.

## Limitations
- Danish perspective; some recommendations may not fit Kathmandu’s budget or politics.
- More about design than traffic behaviour modelling.

## Suggested use
Use as inspiration for observational methods and for framing your study around pedestrian quality of life.
