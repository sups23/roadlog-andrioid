# Papadimitriou, Yannis & Golias — A Critical Assessment of Pedestrian Behaviour Models

## Citation
Papadimitriou, E., Yannis, G., & Golias, J. (2012). "A critical assessment of pedestrian behaviour models." *Accident Analysis & Prevention*, 44, 5–19. DOI: 10.1016/j.aap.2010.12.018.

## Core purpose
This paper reviews and critiques models that predict pedestrian crossing behaviour, including gap acceptance, route choice, and compliance models.

## What is useful for intersection observation research
- **Model taxonomy:** Divides pedestrian behaviour into crossing (gap acceptance, street crossing) and route choice.
- **Key variables:** Age, gender, group size, traffic volume, crossing distance, time pressure, and alcohol.
- **Methodological critique:** Identifies weaknesses such as small samples, limited contexts, and lack of validation.
- **Behavioural theories:** Discusses utility maximization, risk homeostasis, and social learning.

## Key takeaways for a Kathmandu project
- Helps you justify which variables to measure (age, gender, group size, waiting time, gap size).
- Shows that most models come from developed countries; your local study fills a gap.
- Useful literature-review framing for a master’s thesis.

## Limitations
- Review paper, not a primary observation study.
- Some models are mathematically complex.

## Suggested use
Cite this in your literature review to establish the state of the art and identify the gap your observational study addresses.
