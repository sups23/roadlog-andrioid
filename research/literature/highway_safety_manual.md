# Highway Safety Manual (HSM)

## Citation
American Association of State Highway and Transportation Officials (AASHTO). *Highway Safety Manual*, 1st edition (2010) and supplements.

## Core purpose
The HSM provides predictive methods for estimating crash frequency and severity on different road facilities, including intersections. It is designed to support safety decision-making.

## What is useful for intersection observation research
- **Safety Performance Functions (SPFs):** Equations that predict expected crash counts based on traffic volume, geometry, and control type.
- **Crash Modification Factors (CMFs):** Quantifies how treatments (e.g., adding a signal, marking a crosswalk) affect safety.
- **Predictive method for intersections:** Step-by-step procedure for estimating pedestrian and vehicle crashes at signalized and unsignalized intersections.

## Key takeaways for a Kathmandu project
- Provides a safety-oriented framing: your observed conflicts can be treated as proxies for crash risk.
- Helps argue that small observational studies are valuable when official crash data are incomplete or unreliable.
- Offers vocabulary: exposure, conflict, severity, surrogate safety measure.

## Limitations
- Calibrated primarily with US crash data; direct transfer to Kathmandu is questionable.
- Requires detailed geometric and volume data that may be hard to obtain.

## Suggested use
Use the HSM to frame your observational conflict counts as surrogate safety indicators, not to claim precise crash predictions.
