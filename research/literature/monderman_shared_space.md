# Monderman — Shared Space Concept

## Citation
Monderman, H. (various publications, 1990s–2000s). Search: "Hans Monderman shared space" or "shared space traffic calming."

## Core purpose
Monderman developed the “shared space” approach, in which road users (drivers, cyclists, pedestrians) negotiate space through eye contact and social interaction rather than relying on signs, signals, and markings.

## What is useful for intersection observation research
- **Informal negotiation:** How people behave when formal rules are removed or absent.
- **Eye contact and gestures:** Communication methods you can observe.
- **Traffic calming:** How design can slow vehicles and increase awareness.
- **Cultural context:** Shared space resembles everyday behaviour at many Kathmandu intersections.

## Key takeaways for a Kathmandu project
- Helps explain why Kathmandu intersections often function as “shared space” by default.
- Supports observation of informal rules and mutual adjustment.
- Useful for topics on uncontrolled intersections and negotiation behaviour.

## Limitations
- Controversial; critics note accessibility and safety concerns for vulnerable users.
- Originally designed for low-speed, low-volume European town centres.

## Suggested use
Use as a conceptual lens. Frame your observed intersections as informal shared spaces where users negotiate priority.
