# Fitzpatrick et al. — Pedestrian Gap Acceptance

## Citation
Fitzpatrick, K., Turner, S., & Brewer, M. (2006). *Pedestrian Gap Acceptance*. FHWA-RD-99-093. Federal Highway Administration, U.S. Department of Transportation.

## Core purpose
This FHWA report is one of the most cited studies on pedestrian gap acceptance in the United States. It measures the size of traffic gaps that pedestrians are willing to accept before crossing at uncontrolled locations.

## What is useful for intersection observation research
- **Critical gap:** The minimum time gap between vehicles that a pedestrian will accept.
- **Methodology:** How to record rejected gaps, accepted gaps, and waiting time from video data.
- **Influencing factors:** Effects of crossing distance, number of lanes, vehicle speed, and pedestrian age.
- **Walking speed:** Typical crossing speeds for different pedestrian groups.

## Key takeaways for a Kathmandu project
- You can replicate the gap-acceptance method with a stopwatch and notebook.
- Measure the time gap between vehicles and note whether the pedestrian crossed.
- Compare accepted gaps across groups (students, elderly, women, groups vs. singles).

## Limitations
- Based on US traffic conditions with lower pedestrian density and higher rule compliance.
- Does not address group crossing, negotiation gestures, or mixed two-wheeler traffic.

## Suggested use
Use this report to define your gap-acceptance variables and cite it as the foundational method for measuring pedestrian risk tolerance.
