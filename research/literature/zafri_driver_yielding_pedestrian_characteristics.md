# Zafri, Tabassum, Himal, Sultana & Debnath — Driver Yielding and Pedestrian Characteristics

## Citation
Zafri, N. M., Tabassum, T., Himal, M. R. H., Sultana, R., & Debnath, A. K. (2022). "Effect of pedestrian characteristics and their road crossing behaviors on driver yielding behavior at controlled intersections." *Journal of Safety Research*, 81, 1–8. DOI: 10.1016/j.jsr.2022.01.001.

## Core purpose
The study examines how pedestrian characteristics and crossing behaviours influence whether drivers yield at controlled intersections in Bangladesh.

## What is useful for intersection observation research
- **Driver yielding as outcome:** A binary variable that is easy to observe: did the vehicle slow/stop for the pedestrian?
- **Pedestrian predictors:** Age, gender, group size, crossing location, and signal compliance.
- **South Asian context:** Conducted in Bangladesh, with traffic conditions relatively close to Kathmandu.
- **Statistical model:** Logistic regression linking pedestrian actions to driver response.

## Key takeaways for a Kathmandu project
- You can code every pedestrian-vehicle interaction as “yielded” or “did not yield.”
- Pedestrian behaviour affects driver behaviour, not only the reverse.
- A very practical topic: “What makes drivers yield to pedestrians in Kathmandu?”

## Limitations
- Focus on controlled intersections; may need adaptation for unsignalized ones.
- Requires observing many interactions for statistical power.

## Suggested use
Use this as the central reference if your topic is driver-pedestrian negotiation or pedestrian safety at crossings.
