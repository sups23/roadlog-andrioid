# Zegeer et al. — Safety Effects of Marked vs. Unmarked Crosswalks

## Citation
Zegeer, C. V., Stewart, J. R., Huang, H. H., & Lagerwey, P. A. (2005). "Safety effects of marked vs. unmarked crosswalks at uncontrolled locations.” *Transportation Research Record*, 1878(1), 56–68. DOI: 10.3141/1878-10.

## Core purpose
This influential study compares crash rates and pedestrian behaviour at marked and unmarked crosswalks across US cities.

## What is useful for intersection observation research
- **Marked vs. unmarked comparison:** Tests whether zebra crossings actually make pedestrians safer.
- **Context sensitivity:** Finds that on multi-lane roads with high traffic, marked crosswalks alone may not improve safety.
- **Crash prediction:** Identifies road characteristics associated with higher pedestrian crash risk.

## Key takeaways for a Kathmandu project
- Directly supports a comparison topic: zebra crossing use and safety at one or more intersections.
- Suggests that markings alone are not enough; driver yielding and traffic speed matter.
- You can replicate the comparison locally by observing compliance and conflicts at marked vs. unmarked locations.

## Limitations
- US data; traffic culture is different.
- Focuses on crashes, not micro-behaviours.

## Suggested use
Cite this when arguing that simply painting a zebra crossing does not guarantee safe behaviour.
