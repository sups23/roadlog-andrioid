# Hydén — The Swedish Traffic Conflicts Technique

## Citation
Hydén, C. (1987). *The Development of a Method for Traffic Safety Evaluation: The Swedish Traffic Conflicts Technique*. Lund, Sweden: Lund Institute of Technology, Department of Traffic Planning and Engineering.

## Core purpose
Hydén developed a systematic method to observe and classify “traffic conflicts” — near-miss situations where a crash would have occurred if one road user had not taken evasive action.

## What is useful for intersection observation research
- **Conflict definition:** A standardized way to identify events where two road users are on a collision course.
- **Severity grading:** Conflicts can be graded by how close the encounter is and how severe evasive action is required.
- **Surrogate safety measure:** Conflicts are used as a proxy for actual crashes, which are rare and hard to observe directly.
- **Evasive actions:** Braking, swerving, horn use, and sudden stops are observable indicators.

## Key takeaways for a Kathmandu project
- Allows you to measure safety without waiting for accident records.
- Conflicts between bikes/scooters and pedestrians are common in mixed traffic and easy to observe.
- You can count conflicts per hour and compare across locations or times of day.

## Limitations
- Subjective: different observers may classify the same event differently.
- Requires training and clear operational definitions.
- Less validated in mixed-traffic, low-rule environments.

## Suggested use
Adopt Hydén’s conflict concept for your observation sheet. Define a conflict as any event where a pedestrian and vehicle adjust speed/direction to avoid contact.
