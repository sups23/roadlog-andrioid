# Pawar & Patil — Pedestrian Temporal and Spatial Gap Acceptance at Mid-Block Crossings

## Citation
Pawar, D. S., & Patil, G. R. "Pedestrian temporal and spatial gap acceptance at mid-block street crossing in developing world." (Search ResearchGate / Semantic Scholar for exact publication details.)

## Core purpose
The study investigates both temporal (time) and spatial (distance) gap acceptance of pedestrians crossing mid-block locations in developing countries.

## What is useful for intersection observation research
- **Two dimensions of gap:** Pedestrians judge both the time between vehicles and the physical distance available.
- **Driver yielding:** Notes that driver yielding behaviour strongly affects pedestrian gap acceptance.
- **Developing-world context:** Explicitly addresses the higher pedestrian density and more chaotic vehicle flow typical of South Asian cities.
- **Safety implications:** Provides guidance for designing safer mid-block crossings.

## Key takeaways for a Kathmandu project
- You can record both temporal gaps (seconds) and spatial gaps (metres or vehicle lengths).
- Driver yielding is a key variable in Kathmandu; count yielding and non-yielding vehicles.
- Mid-block and intersection crossings share many behavioural principles.

## Limitations
- Focus on mid-block crossings rather than intersections.
- Need to verify exact publication details and data source.

## Suggested use
Use to justify measuring both time and space when observing pedestrian crossing decisions.
