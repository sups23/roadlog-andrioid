# Application of Gap Acceptance Concept — 42 Indian Cities Study

## Citation
Search title: "Application of Gap Acceptance Concept to Investigate Crossing Behaviour of Pedestrians at Unsignalized Intersections." Study covering 42 unsignalized intersections in seven Indian cities.

## Core purpose
The study investigates pedestrian gap acceptance behaviour at unsignalized intersections across multiple Indian cities to establish safety margins for crossing in mixed traffic.

## What is useful for intersection observation research
- **Large sample:** 42 intersections in seven cities, providing broader generalizability than single-site studies.
- **Mixed traffic context:** Captures the behaviour of pedestrians among heterogeneous vehicles (bikes, scooters, cars, buses, trucks).
- **Regression approach:** Models gap acceptance as a function of road geometry, traffic flow, and pedestrian characteristics.
- **Safety margins:** Establishes critical gaps for local conditions.

## Key takeaways for a Kathmandu project
- Shows that gap acceptance studies are feasible and publishable in South Asian mixed traffic.
- Demonstrates value of collecting data at multiple intersections rather than one.
- Road geometry and vehicle mix are important control variables.

## Limitations
- The exact authors/year should be verified from the source.
- Indian traffic conditions differ somewhat from Kathmandu, though less so than Western cities.

Use as evidence that your topic fits an established research stream in South Asia. Adapt the multi-site design if you have time.
