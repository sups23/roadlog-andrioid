# Tiwari, Bangdivala, Saraswat & Gaurav — Survival Analysis of Pedestrian Risk Exposure

## Citation
Tiwari, G., Bangdivala, S., Saraswat, A., & Gaurav, S. (2007). "Survival analysis: Pedestrian risk exposure at signalized intersections.” *Accident Analysis & Prevention*, 39(6), 1161–1168. DOI: 10.1016/j.aap.2007.03.006.

## Core purpose
The study applies survival analysis to measure how long pedestrians are exposed to traffic while crossing signalized intersections in India.

## What is useful for intersection observation research
- **Risk exposure time:** Measures the time pedestrians spend on the road and in conflict with vehicles.
- **Survival analysis:** A statistical method borrowed from medical research, adapted for crossing behaviour.
- **Indian context:** Directly relevant to mixed traffic, weak lane discipline, and high pedestrian volume.
- **Influencing factors:** Crossing length, pedestrian speed, traffic volume, and signal timing.

## Key takeaways for a Kathmandu project
- You can measure “exposure time” with a stopwatch: from when a pedestrian steps off the curb until they reach the other side.
- Longer exposure means higher risk.
- Demonstrates that behavioural studies in South Asian cities need locally adapted methods.

## Limitations
- Requires video data for precise timing.
- Statistical method may need adaptation for small samples.

## Suggested use
Use this to frame a topic like “Pedestrian exposure time and conflict risk at intersections in Kathmandu.”
