# Hauer — Observational Before-After Studies in Road Safety

## Citation
Hauer, E. (1997). *Observational Before-After Studies in Road Safety*. Oxford: Pergamon / Elsevier.

## Core purpose
Hauer’s book explains how to evaluate road safety changes using observational data, especially when randomized experiments are impossible. It addresses biases such as regression-to-the-mean and traffic volume changes.

## What is useful for intersection observation research
- **Before-after design:** How to compare the same intersection before and after a treatment (e.g., new zebra paint, new signal, police deployment).
- **Comparison group design:** How to use a similar untreated intersection as a control.
- **Empirical Bayes method:** A statistical approach to adjust for random fluctuation in crash/conflict counts.

## Key takeaways for a Kathmandu project
- If a local intersection is about to receive a treatment, you can design a natural experiment.
- Even without a treatment, you can compare two similar intersections (e.g., one with zebra, one without) using cross-sectional logic.
- Helps you avoid over-interpreting short-term fluctuations in observed conflicts.

## Limitations
- Methodologically demanding; full Empirical Bayes may be beyond a small master’s project.
- Designed for crash counts, not behavioural observations.

## Suggested use
Read the early chapters for study-design principles. Use simple before-after or matched-location comparisons if your topic allows.
