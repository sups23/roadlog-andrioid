# Vanderbilt — Traffic: Why We Drive the Way We Do

## Citation
Vanderbilt, T. (2008). *Traffic: Why We Drive the Way We Do (and What It Says About Us)*. New York: Alfred A. Knopf.

## Core purpose
A popular-science exploration of the psychology, sociology, and engineering behind driving behaviour.

## What is useful for intersection observation research
- **Risk perception:** Why drivers and pedestrians misjudge danger.
- **Cultural differences:** How traffic norms vary across countries.
- **Distracted driving:** Phone use, multitasking, and inattention.
- **Speed and safety:** Why faster roads feel safer but are more dangerous.

## Key takeaways for a Kathmandu project
- Helps explain why observed behaviours (e.g., jaywalking, red-light running) make sense to the people doing them.
- Readable source for framing human mentality and risk.
- Good for non-academic readers and thesis introductions.

## Limitations
- Not an academic research methods text.
- Mostly focused on driving in developed countries.

## Suggested use
Use for background and motivation, especially if your topic touches on risk psychology or cultural norms.
