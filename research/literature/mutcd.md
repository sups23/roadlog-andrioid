# Manual on Uniform Traffic Control Devices (MUTCD)

## Citation
Federal Highway Administration (FHWA). *Manual on Uniform Traffic Control Devices for Streets and Highways*. Current edition.

## Core purpose
MUTCD defines the standards for traffic signs, signals, pavement markings, and crosswalks in the United States. It specifies when and where crosswalks, stop lines, signals, and other controls should be installed.

## What is useful for intersection observation research
- **Crosswalk definitions:** Distinguishes between marked and unmarked crosswalks, school crosswalks, and high-visibility treatments.
- **Sign/signal warrants:** Criteria for installing signals or stop controls; helps explain why a particular intersection does or does not have formal control.
- **Compliance variables:** Provides the legal/rule basis for counting violations (e.g., crossing against a red signal, vehicles failing to yield).
- **Standard markings:** Describes zebra crossings, stop lines, pedestrian signals, and countdown timers.

## Key takeaways for a Kathmandu project
- Gives you a formal benchmark for “rule compliance.”
- Helps justify measuring zebra-crossing use vs. non-use.
- Useful background if you compare signalized vs. unsignalized intersections.

## Limitations
- MUTCD reflects American traffic culture and design standards; many Kathmandu intersections will not conform to these assumptions.
- It does not address informal negotiation or mixed-traffic conditions.

## Suggested use
Cite MUTCD when defining what counts as a marked crosswalk, a violation, or a standard pedestrian facility, then show the gap between design intent and actual behaviour.
