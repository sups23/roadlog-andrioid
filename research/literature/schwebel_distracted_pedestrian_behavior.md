# Schwebel et al. — Distracted Pedestrian Behaviour

## Citation
Schwebel, D. C., Canter, M. F., Hasan, R., Griffin, R., White, T. R., & Johnston, A. (2022). "Distracted pedestrian behavior: An observational study of risk by situational environments." *Traffic Injury Prevention*, 23(6), 346–351. DOI: 10.1080/15389588.2022.2076282.

## Core purpose
The study observes distracted walking (especially phone use) and its association with unsafe crossing behaviour in different environmental contexts.

## What is useful for intersection observation research
- **Distraction coding:** Easy to observe categories: talking on phone, texting, wearing earphones, no distraction.
- **Unsafe crossing:** Crossing against signals, outside crosswalks, or without looking.
- **Environment matters:** Risky behaviour varies by intersection type and traffic volume.
- **Large observational sample:** Demonstrates feasibility of behavioural coding in public space.

## Key takeaways for a Kathmandu project
- Phone use while crossing is visible and increasing in Kathmandu.
- You can add a simple “distracted yes/no” column to your observation sheet.
- Connects to your interest in human mentality and risk-taking.

## Limitations
- US context; phone-use norms and traffic dangers differ.
- Distracted pedestrians may be harder to spot in very dense crowds.

## Suggested use
Use for a topic like “Mobile phone distraction and risky crossing behaviour at Kathmandu intersections.”
