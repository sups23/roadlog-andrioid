# Noonan, Gershon, Domeyer, Mehler & Reimer — Interdependence of Driver and Pedestrian Behaviour

## Citation
Noonan, T. Z., Gershon, P., Domeyer, J., Mehler, B., & Reimer, B. (2022). "Interdependence of driver and pedestrian behavior in naturalistic roadway negotiations." *Traffic Injury Prevention*, 23(sup1), S62–S67. DOI: 10.1080/15389588.2022.2108023.

## Core purpose
The study analyzes naturalistic driving data to show how driver and pedestrian behaviours mutually influence each other during crossing negotiations.

## What is useful for intersection observation research
- **Mutual influence:** Drivers adjust speed based on pedestrian cues; pedestrians adjust timing based on vehicle cues.
- **Group effects:** Crossing group size affects driver behaviour.
- **Lead pedestrian:** First pedestrian in a group influences the interaction.
- **Naturalistic data:** Real-world behaviour rather than simulator or lab.

## Key takeaways for a Kathmandu project
- Supports a dynamic view: crossing is a negotiation, not a one-sided decision.
- You can observe mutual adjustments (pedestrian hesitates → vehicle slows → pedestrian crosses).
- Group crossing is again highlighted as important.

## Limitations
- Uses instrumented vehicles in the US; different traffic density and rule compliance.
- Focuses on kinematic data more than demographic or contextual factors.

## Suggested use
Use to frame crossing as a social interaction. Code sequences of action-reaction between pedestrians and vehicles.
