# Highway Capacity Manual (HCM 2010 / 2016)

## Citation
Transportation Research Board (TRB). *Highway Capacity Manual*, 2010 / 2016 editions. Washington, DC: National Academies.

## Core purpose
The HCM is the standard reference for measuring and rating the performance of streets, intersections, and facilities in the United States. It is widely used internationally as a methodological baseline.

## What is useful for intersection observation research
- **Pedestrian Level of Service (LOS):** A method to rate pedestrian quality of service at intersections using delay, space, and conflict measures.
- **Pedestrian delay:** Procedures for estimating control delay at signalized and unsignalized intersections.
- **Crossing difficulty:** Concepts for pedestrian crossing time, walking speed assumptions, and vehicle-pedestrian conflicts.
- **Volume and flow definitions:** Standard definitions for peak-hour factor, flow rates, and directional splits.

## Key takeaways for a Kathmandu project
- Provides legitimacy: you can adopt standard variables such as delay, waiting time, and LOS.
- Shows how developed-country manuals assume orderly lane discipline and signal compliance — which makes the Kathmandu mixed-traffic case a useful contrast.
- Useful for defining your observation variables (e.g., average pedestrian delay, number of conflict events per hour).

## Limitations
- Designed for regulated, lane-based traffic; many procedures must be adapted for mixed, chaotic, or unregulated flows.
- Does not address informal behaviours such as negotiation gestures, group crossing dynamics, or non-compliance cultures.

## Suggested use
Use HCM definitions as a starting framework, then explain how your local context requires modifications.
