# Noonan, Gershon, Domeyer, Mehler & Reimer — Kinematic Cues in Driver-Pedestrian Communication

## Citation
Noonan, T. Z., Gershon, P., Domeyer, J., Mehler, B., & Reimer, B. (2023). "Kinematic cues in driver-pedestrian communication to support safe road crossing." *Accident Analysis & Prevention*, 192, 107236. DOI: 10.1016/j.aap.2023.107236.

## Core purpose
The study examines how vehicle acceleration and deceleration patterns communicate intent to pedestrians, and how pedestrians use these kinematic cues to decide when to cross.

## What is useful for intersection observation research
- **Vehicle deceleration as communication:** A slowing vehicle signals yielding; maintaining speed signals “do not cross.”
- **Information peak:** The point at which vehicle motion gives the clearest signal to pedestrians.
- **Crossing location effect:** Cues differ at designated crossings vs. mid-block.
- **Safety design implications:** How automated vehicles might communicate with pedestrians.

## Key takeaways for a Kathmandu project
- You can observe whether vehicles slow before reaching the pedestrian or only at the last moment.
- Early deceleration may lead to smoother, safer crossings.
- Adds a subtle but observable variable to your study.

## Limitations
- US-based with instrumented vehicles.
- Requires precise speed/decision timing data.

## Suggested use
Use if your topic includes driver-pedestrian communication. Code “early yield,” “late yield,” and “no yield” as categories.
