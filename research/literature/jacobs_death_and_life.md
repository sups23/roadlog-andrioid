# Jacobs — The Death and Life of Great American Cities

## Citation
Jacobs, J. (1961). *The Death and Life of Great American Cities*. New York: Random House.

## Core purpose
A foundational critique of modernist urban planning and a defence of dense, mixed-use, pedestrian-oriented streets.

## What is useful for intersection observation research
- **Eyes on the street:** How continuous pedestrian activity improves safety.
- **Mixed use:** Why streets with shops, homes, and transport create vitality.
- **Pedestrian perspective:** Cities should be designed for people, not only vehicles.
- **Informal order:** How unplanned social behaviours produce urban order.

## Key takeaways for a Kathmandu project
- Encourages you to see intersections as social spaces, not just traffic nodes.
- Supports observation of informal behaviours (vendors, loitering, negotiation).
- Useful for arguing that local, pedestrian-level knowledge matters.

## Limitations
- Focused on mid-20th-century American cities.
- Not a transport engineering manual.

## Suggested use
Use for theoretical framing if your project emphasizes pedestrians, public space, or informal urban life.
