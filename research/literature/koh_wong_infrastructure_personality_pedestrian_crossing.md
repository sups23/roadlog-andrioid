# Koh & Wong — Influences of Infrastructure and Personality on Pedestrian Crossing Behaviour

## Citation
Koh, P. P., & Wong, Y. D. (2014). "Influences of infrastructure and personality on pedestrian crossing behaviour.” *Accident Analysis & Prevention*, 62, 57–63. DOI: 10.1016/j.aap.2013.10.015.

## Core purpose
The study examines how both physical infrastructure and individual personality traits influence pedestrian crossing decisions at signalized intersections in Singapore.

## What is useful for intersection observation research
- **Infrastructure factors:** Crossing distance, signal timing, presence of medians, and markings.
- **Personality traits:** Sensation-seeking, impulsiveness, and risk-taking tendencies.
- **Observed behaviours:** Crossing against the signal, crossing outside the crosswalk, and distracted walking.

## Key takeaways for a Kathmandu project
- Useful if you want to combine infrastructure observations with simple intercept surveys about risk attitude.
- Shows that road design and individual mentality interact.
- Relevant to your interest in human mentality.

## Limitations
- Singapore has stricter traffic rule compliance than Kathmandu.
- Personality requires survey data, not pure observation.

## Suggested use
Use for topics that link intersection design to observed risk-taking. Consider a short survey alongside your observation.
