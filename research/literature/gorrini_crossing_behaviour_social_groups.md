# Gorrini, Crociani, Vizzari & Bandini — Crossing Behaviour of Social Groups

## Citation
Gorrini, A., Crociani, L., Vizzari, G., & Bandini, S. (2017). "Crossing Behaviour of Social Groups: Insights from Observations at Non-signalized Intersection." *Proceedings of the 12th International Conference on Traffic and Granular Flow (TGF 2017)*, Washington, DC. Also available on arXiv:1711.00728.

## Core purpose
The study investigates how pedestrians behave when crossing in pairs (dyads) compared to alone at non-signalized intersections.

## What is useful for intersection observation research
- **Three-phase crossing model:** (1) approaching, (2) appraising/decision-making, (3) crossing.
- **Group dynamics:** Dyads walk slower than singles in all three phases.
- **Leadership:** One person in a dyad typically decides to cross first; the other follows.
- **Safety gap:** Interestingly, accepted safety gaps were not significantly different between singles and dyads.

## Key takeaways for a Kathmandu project
- You can classify pedestrians as single, pair, or group and compare their crossing behaviour.
- The “leader-follower” pattern is easy to observe and highly relevant to South Asian group crossings.
- The three-phase model gives you a natural coding structure for your observation sheet.

## Limitations
- Conducted in Italy (Milan); cultural context differs from Kathmandu.
- Small sample focused on social groups, not all demographic types.

## Suggested use
Adopt the three-phase model as your analytical framework. Add demographic variables (age, gender, student status) to extend the analysis.
