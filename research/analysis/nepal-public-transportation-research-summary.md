# Nepal Public Transportation Research: Comprehensive Summary

## Source: IOE Graduate Conference Proceedings & Tribhuvan University eLibrary

---

## 1. Banasthali-Sundhara (Reference Paper)

**Title:** "Determining the Impact of Public Transportation on Urban Mobility: A Case Study of Banasthali-Sundhara Urban Road"
**Authors:** Birat Sharma & Padma Bahadur Shahi
**Year:** 2021 | **Source:** IOEGC-10, Pages 504-513

### Key Findings
- **Public transport is in critical condition** in Kathmandu: no fixed schedules, poorly maintained vehicles (Kia/Chinese micro buses with insufficient headroom), bus stops occupied by vendors.
- **Demand overlap:** Most institutions share similar working hours, creating concentrated peak-hour surges.
- **Intersection bottlenecks:** Vehicle crossing times at Balaju, Sorakhutte, and Keshar Mall intersections reached up to **7.34 minutes** during peak hours.
- **Micro buses avg. 19.94 km/hr** vs. mini buses at **12.61 km/hr**; private vehicles cover the route in ~16 min vs. 22 min by micro bus.
- **Queue lengths:** Up to 210m at Balaju Chowk, 160m at Sorakhutte, 142m at Keshar Mall during peak hours.
- **Pedestrian infrastructure is poor:** Missing footpaths (Banasthali-Balaju section), vendors occupying bus stops, no shelters, damaged walkways.
- **User survey (n=115):** 46% walk 11-15 min to bus stops; 43% use public transport for employment; 36% cite no fixed schedule as the biggest problem.
- **Parking problems:** On-street parking dominates, rendering 4-lane roads to single-lane in practice.

### Recommendations
- Large low-floor energy-efficient buses; enforce schedules with high penalties; improve footpaths (min 5' width); bicycle lane integration; end single-owner private public transport vehicles; subsidized fares for disadvantaged groups.

## 2. Strategies for Sustainable Urban Mobility: Dharan

**Title:** "Strategies for Sustainable Urban Mobility: A Case Dharan Sub-Metropolitan City"
**Authors:** Suman Limbu & Padma Bahadur Shahi
**Year:** 2020 | **Source:** IOEGC-8, Pages 615-619
**PDF:** `conference.ioe.edu.np/ioegc8/papers/ioegc-8-075-80102.pdf`

### Key Focus
- Studies urban mobility in **Dharan** (a growing sub-metropolitan city outside Kathmandu), contrasting with the dominant Kathmandu-centric research.
- Uses survey questionnaires to determine existing urban mobility issues and propose sustainable strategies for transportation planning.
- Focuses on **understanding people's opinions** regarding existing transport systems to gain appropriate decisions toward adequate planning.

### Significance
- Shifts focus from Kathmandu Valley to **secondary cities** in Nepal that are experiencing rapid urbanization but lack adequate public transport planning.
- Demonstrates that urban mobility challenges are not unique to Kathmandu but are a **nationwide urban planning concern**.

## 3. Passengers' Perception of Public Transportation Quality: Kathmandu Valley

**Title:** "Passengers' Perception towards Quality of Public Transportation Services in Kathmandu Valley"
**Authors:** Samiksha Sharma & Rojee Pradhananga
**Year:** 2021 | **Source:** IOEGC-10, Pages 596-603
**PDF:** `conference.ioe.edu.np/publications/ioegc10/ioegc-10-077-10107.pdf`

### Key Focus
- Detailed study on **passenger perception** of public transportation service quality.
- Identifies a **prioritized list of features requiring immediate improvement**.
- Fills a gap in Nepali literature by systematically studying user-side quality metrics.
- Uses satisfaction surveys and quality assessment frameworks to derive improvement priorities.

### Significance
- Complements the Banasthali-Sundhara paper by providing a **user-centered quality assessment framework** rather than a corridor-specific traffic study.
- Helps policymakers prioritize interventions based on what passengers actually value most.

## 4. Active Mobility in Historical Core: Kathmandu

**Title:** "Analysis of Active Mobility in the Historical Core City of Kathmandu"
**Authors:** Manish Maharjan & Padma B. Shahi
**Year:** 2021 | **Source:** IOEGC-10, Pages 558-562
**PDF:** `conference.ioe.edu.np/ioegc10/papers/ioegc-10-072-10098.pdf`

### Key Focus
- Analyzes factors affecting **active mobility** (walking and cycling) in Kathmandu's historical core areas.
- Research follows post-positivist paradigm with triangulation of: literature review, related case studies, and site observation.
- Identifies ways to **improve active mobility** in heritage-sensitive urban contexts.

### Significance
- Addresses an often-neglected aspect of urban mobility: **non-motorized transport** in dense historical areas.
- Relevant for cities like Kathmandu where narrow streets in core areas make traditional public transport expansion difficult.
- Links **heritage conservation** with transportation planning.

## 5. Gender-Responsive Urban Mobility: Lagankhel to Jamal

**Title:** "Gender Responsive Urban Mobility Planning (A Case of Lagankhel to Jamal)"
**Year:** 2019 | **Source:** IOEGC Summer 2019
**PDF:** `conference.ioe.edu.np/ioegc2019-summer/papers/IOEGC-2019-Summer-063.pdf`

### Key Focus
- Examines **gender-specific mobility issues** along the Lagankhel to Jamal corridor.
- Uses a framework matrix for analyzing mobility issues experienced by **women** while using public transport (Sajha Yatayat chosen as case study) and while walking.
- Observes and surveys gender-specific safety, comfort, and accessibility concerns.

### Significance
- First gender-focused mobility study in the IOE conference series.
- Brings attention to **gendered dimensions** of public transport: safety concerns, harassment, design of vehicles/stops for women's needs.
- Aligns with global sustainable development goals (SDG 5 - Gender Equality, SDG 11 - Sustainable Cities).

## 7. Travel Time Reliability: Airport to Narayan Gopal Chowk

**Title:** "Modelling Travel Time Reliability of Public Bus Transport: A Case Study of Airport to Narayan Gopal Chowk Route, Kathmandu"
**Authors:** Santosh Kumar Thapa & Rojee Pradhananga
**Year:** 2023 | **Source:** IOEGC-14, Pages 451-457
**PDF:** `conference.ioe.edu.np/publications/ioegc14/IOEGC-14-068-D3-4-620.pdf`

### Key Focus
- Analyzes **95% travel time reliability** for a bus route in Kathmandu.
- Evaluates reliability factors per **India's National Urban Transport Policy Service Level Benchmarks**: headway, passenger waiting time, travel speed, journey time.
- Addresses the challenge of data-scarce environments typical of developing cities.

### Significance
- One of the few studies applying **quantitative reliability modeling** to Nepali public transport.
- Introduces **travel time reliability** as a critical metric (beyond just average speed/travel time).
- Highlights the challenge of limited data availability in Nepali transport planning.

## 8. Efficiency and Satisfaction: Kathmandu Inner Ring Road

**Title:** "Study on Efficiency and Satisfaction Level of Public Transportation within Kathmandu Inner Ring Road"
**Author:** Bishnu Bhattarai
**Year:** ~2014 | **Source:** TU Thesis No. T09/070, Pulchowk Campus
**Download:** `elibrary.tucl.edu.np/bitstreams/a0032991-fc54-4ce3-864f-5a63d1312f20/download`

### Key Focus
- Assesses both **operational efficiency** and **user satisfaction** of public transport within Kathmandu's inner ring road area.
- Uses a two-pronged approach: efficiency metrics (travel time, frequency, occupancy) combined with satisfaction surveys.
- Covers the most densely served public transport area of Kathmandu.

### Significance
- An early benchmark study for public transport performance in the inner ring road.
- Combines **supply-side (efficiency) and demand-side (satisfaction)** analyses in one study.

## 9. Transport Mode Choice: Kathmandu Valley

**Title:** "Analysis of Transport Mode Choice in Kathmandu Valley, Nepal: Lessons"
**Source:** `elibrary.tucl.edu.np/items/3a584875-726a-4ca6-932c-95436e9a6319`

### Key Focus
- **Travel demand analysis** focusing on mode choice modeling.
- Helps understand the **decision-making process** of travelers choosing between different transport modes.
- Links mode choice to creating suitable actions for achieving **sustainable transport**.
- Addresses the link between transportation, mobility, and environmental sustainability.

### Significance
- Mode choice modeling is fundamental for **transport demand forecasting** and policy simulation.
- Understanding why people choose private vehicles over public transport is essential for designing effective interventions.

## 10. Public Transport Integration: Pokhara Metropolitan City

**Title:** "An Assessment of Public Transport System from the Integration Perspective: A Case Study of Pokhara Metropolitan City"
**Authors:** Dipesh Thapa & Padma Bahadur Shahi
**Year:** 2025 | **Source:** TU eLibrary (Architecture Engineering collection)

### Key Focus
- Assesses public transport from an **integration perspective** in Pokhara.
- Examines how different modes, routes, and services are (or are not) integrated.
- Likely addresses inter-modal connectivity, fare integration, scheduling coordination, and physical infrastructure integration.

### Significance
- Pokhara is Nepal's second-largest city and major tourist destination with unique transport needs.
- **Integration** is a key concept in modern public transport planning—this study addresses a critical gap.
- One of the most recent studies (2025), reflecting current challenges.

## Cross-Cutting Themes Across All Studies

### 1. System Inefficiency
Across studies, public transport in Nepal is described as **inefficient, unreliable, and uncomfortable**. Common problems include:
- No fixed schedules or timetables
- Old, poorly maintained vehicles with insufficient capacity
- Vehicles stopping anywhere (not at designated stops) to pick up passengers
- Competition among operators leading to dangerous overtaking and stopping behavior
- Single-owner vehicle model creating fragmented operations

### 2. Infrastructure Deficits
- **Bus stops** are undersized, lack shelters, or are occupied by vendors
- **Footpaths** are damaged, missing, too narrow, or used for parking/garbage
- **Road conditions** are poor despite being blacktopped four-lane roads
- **Zebra crossings** and pedestrian safety infrastructure are inadequate
- **No dedicated bicycle lanes** or segregated NMT infrastructure

### 3. Demand-Side Issues
- **Demand overlap** due to uniform working hours across institutions
- Growing preference for **private vehicles** (motorcycles and cars) due to poor public transport
- Public transport seen as a mode of last resort rather than a preferred choice
- **Gender-specific concerns** about safety and harassment
- **Accessibility barriers** for elderly and disabled persons

### 4. Policy and Planning Gaps
- **No comprehensive national public transport policy** framework
- Unsustainable fare/tariff control systems
- Lack of **integration** between different modes and routes
- Inadequate data collection and transport planning capacity
- NGO and private sector fragmented approach without government coordination

### 5. Environmental and Social Dimensions
- Air pollution from vehicle emissions is a growing health concern
- Congestion leading to economic losses (lost productivity, fuel wastage)
- Social inequity: poor and disadvantaged most affected by poor public transport
- Urban sprawl exacerbated by lack of efficient mass transit

### Recurring Author: Padma Bahadur Shahi
Notably, Padma Bahadur Shahi (Department of Architecture, Pulchowk Campus) is a co-author on **four** of these studies (Papers 1, 2, 4, 10), making him a key researcher in this field in Nepal.

### Research Methodology Trends
- Most studies use **mixed methods**: questionnaire surveys + on-board observations + secondary data
- Sample sizes range from ~100-200 respondents
- Route-level case study approach dominates rather than city-wide analysis
- Heavy reliance on **manual data collection** (stopwatch timing, manual counting) rather than automated systems
- Most studies are **cross-sectional** (single point in time) rather than longitudinal
